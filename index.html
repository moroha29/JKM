Unit 01

RexSG

Product · Geospatial · PostGIS

HDB Match: a live map for one of Singapore's biggest decisions.

3
property modes (resale, BTO, private)
4
weighted match factors
1
live product at rexsg.com
Brief
Turn fragmented housing data into a decision people can understand and defend.
Move
We didn't ask buyers to trust a black-box score. Drag the weight between commute, lifestyle, value, and lease, and the shortlist reorders live, on the same PostGIS-backed data underneath the whole time, so the trade-off is felt, not just reported.
Read the build story

Challenge

Property search shows inventory. Buyers need judgement.

Price, lease, schools, transit, household needs, and future risk sit across different sources. Comparing them by hand is slow and hard to defend to a spouse, a parent, or a bank.

Approach

One map, weights the buyer controls.

A deterministic geospatial engine (resale medians, accessibility scoring, commute and appreciation models) sits under a live map. The buyer sets what matters; the map re-sorts in front of them.

Outcome

A live, public product, not a prototype.

Real resale listings, real filters, and a shortlist a buyer can defend, because every number traces back to the same evidence underneath.

Unit 03

mySOS

Platform · Commerce · Pricing logic

One catalogue running a public storefront and an internal quoting engine.

2
systems sharing one dataset
4
printing methods: DTF/DTG, silkscreen, embroidery, sublimation
1
public price never equals internal cost
Brief
Give a sales team a quoting tool fast enough for a live call, without the public site and the sales floor ever showing the same number.
Move
We kept the public “from” price and the internal quotation cost as two separate fields on the same product record. A salesperson can quote fast and correctly; the public catalogue never has to leak, or lie about, a real cost.
Read the build story

Challenge

One catalogue, two audiences with opposite needs.

Customers need a simple public price. An internal sales team needs exact costs, printing methods, tiered quantities, and margin, for the same products.

Approach

One schema, two views of it.

Every product carries a public display price and a private quotation cost as distinct fields, with printing methods, add-ons, and quantity tiers modelled as their own pure pricing logic.

Outcome

A public storefront and a live quoting tool, from one source of truth.

Change a cost once and both the public catalogue and the sales team's quotes stay correct, and never accidentally swap places.

Unit 04

Website Manager

Platform · Workflow · Operations

Making publishing feel safe enough to hand over, and powerful enough to keep.

5
managed site directions
2×
before / after review on every change
24/7

Brief
Let non-developers update live sites without turning deployment into a leap of faith.
Move
We treated publishing as a durable, reviewable job, not a button that fires and hopes. Every change maps to real content, every release shows its exact before-and-after, and every deployment can resume after an interruption.
Read the build story

Challenge

Visual editing is easy. Trustworthy publishing is not.

Repeated text, simultaneous editors, image ownership, and failed deployments all create ways for a “simple CMS” to quietly publish the wrong thing.

Approach

Make state and consequence visible.

Sandboxed previews bind rendered blocks to canonical content paths. Immutable drafts, field-level merges, role-based access, and pull requests protect the path to production.

Outcome

A compact operations layer with serious foundations.

Encrypted recovery, audit history, deployment health, and retries live behind an interface built for everyday, non-technical use.

A private, client-only system, not publicly viewable.
